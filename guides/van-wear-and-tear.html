Van & LCV Fair Wear & Tear Guide

A plain-English guide to the BVRLA Light Commercial Vehicle (LCV) wear and tear standard, explaining how working vans are judged and how to avoid end-of-lease charges.

How Van Wear & Tear Differs From Cars

Vans work harder than cars, so the BVRLA light commercial standard makes sensible allowances for everyday business use—especially inside the load area and on steps or sills. Even so, damage caused by overloading, loose or unsecured cargo, poor maintenance, or unapproved modifications can still be charged at the end of the lease.

Exterior Bodywork

Outer paintwork, bumpers, and trim are judged much like a car: light marks are fine, while deep scratches, dents over size limits, or roof/crease damage can be charged.

Load & Working Areas

Normal scuffs, light wear, and minor marks from loading/unloading are expected in the load bay and on entry steps, provided panels are not bent, cracked, or holed.

Cab & Fittings

Seats, floor mats, controls, and cab trim should be clean, intact, and free from burns, rips, heavy stains, or damage left behind by removed equipment.

What Is Usually Acceptable vs. Chargeable on a Van

Area Usually Acceptable (No Charge) Likely Chargeable
Load bay & working surfaces Light scuffs, minor scrapes, and normal wear from loading and unloading goods. Dented, buckled, cracked, or holed floors, walls, bulkheads, tailboards, or doors caused by heavy or unsecured loads.
Outer paint, bumpers & dents Light surface scratches (up to 25mm, within panel limits), small stone chips, and minor bumper/rim scuffs. Deep scratches to bare metal/primer, larger dents, roof or crease/swage-line dents, cracked bumpers, or heavy kerbing.
Signwriting, wraps & add-ons Approved fittings in good working order with required safety checks/certificates where applicable. Unremoved livery/signwriting/wraps, paint or body damage from fitting or removing decals/racks/shelving, or broken equipment.
Cab, tyres & equipment Normal cab wear, legal tyre tread (at least 1.6mm) with even wear, and working lights/glass. Burns, tears, or stubborn stains/odours in the cab, illegal/damaged tyres, MOT-failing glass/lights, or missing keys/manuals/cables.

Before You Hand the Van Back

  • Empty and clean both the cab and the load area completely so every panel and floor section can be inspected clearly.
  • Remove signwriting and add-ons (or check with your leasing provider first) and make sure no holes, adhesive residue, or panel distortion are left behind.
  • Check servicing, keys, and kit—keep service records up to date and include all keys, handbooks, spare/inflation kit, and charging cables.
